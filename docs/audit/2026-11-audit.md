## 1. Gist

1. **Audio has a real failure that the logs cannot see.** On the Mac the
   player (ffplay) keeps receiving and decoding, but the headphone output
   turns to crackle and then to permanent digital silence. Seen live on
   2026-11-13 at 00:40:53 (crackle) and 00:43:03 (silence). Cause: ffplay
   is run with no audio queued, and its output callback blocks while it
   waits for the next packet. Details in §4.
2. **The same design also gives a delay that never goes away.** A player
   that starts late, or sits through a network stall, plays everything
   after that late. Measured 2.0 s after one start. §4.3.
3. **A second, separate cause of "no sound": the client was muted.** The
   Mac client's mute toggle (⌘⇧M) was on for 50.7 h of the current session
   and for 33h54m of an earlier one. §5 finding 1.
4. **The Mac runs an older client** (`ba710e8`, Oct 20) than doggoos
   (`a5a0c4c`, Nov 1), and the two checkouts have diverged. §3.
5. **The host side is clean.** 0 audio encoder restarts and no WARNING or
   ERROR line in 5 sessions since Nov 1 (about 169 h).

Danny will try both audio options in §4.5. Start there.

### 4.2 Why (verified unless marked)

- **ffplay holds no reserve.** It is started with `-probesize 32
  -analyzeduration 0 -fflags nobuffer` (`client.py:997-1011`) so that it
  plays within 0.2 s (October fix N9). Each 20 ms Opus frame is played as
  it arrives.
- **Its output callback blocks.** On non-Windows builds ffplay's audio
  callback waits (`frame_queue_peek_readable`) until a decoded frame
  exists. One `sample` of ffplay 35952 at 00:50:51 showed the SDL audio
  thread in that wait in 174 of 176 samples, inside CoreAudio's
  `AQ::API::Queue::CallOutputCallback → PlaybackBufferReadyCallback →
  SDL_PlaybackAudioThreadIterate`. The decoder thread was waiting too
  (175 of 176), and the read thread and UDP thread were idle in their
  waits. So the queues were empty: this player had no backlog.
- **Crackle** is that state: with nothing queued, any late packet leaves
  the output without data for a moment.
- **Permanent silence: cause not proven (inferred).** Ten seconds after a
  stall of about 1 s the output went silent for good while every stage of
  the chain kept working. Best guess: the AudioQueue that SDL3 uses on
  macOS does not recover after its callback blocks that long. Nobody has
  checked this against SDL or Apple documentation.
- **Reserve and delay are the same thing.** The 00:14:54 player started
  2 s late, so it had 2 s queued, and it kept sound for its whole 21
  minutes. The 00:36:21 player started on time and crackled after 4.5
  minutes.
- **Nothing limits the delay either.** ffplay treats `udp:` as realtime:
  it never stops reading, its packet queue has no cap, and with audio as
  the master clock it never skips or speeds up. A late start or a network
  stall becomes delay for the life of that player. Packet loss shortens
  it again, because lost packets are simply not played.

### 4.4 What does not help

- **The client watchdog.** Both versions watch ffplay's own output (the
  status line on the Mac's version, a decoded-frame heartbeat in
  `feb269d`). Both sit before the sound card, so a player that decodes
  into a dead output looks healthy. The client's `silencedetect` has the
  same blind spot.
- **`rw_timeout=5000000` in the URL.** ffmpeg's UDP input ignores it (§5
  finding 7).
- **Restarting the player.** It brings sound back, and whether the new
  player starts with 0 s or 2 s of delay is luck.

### 4.5 The two options Danny will try

**Option 1 — play audio inside the client (recommended).**
- Decode the same stream with PyAV (already used for video): open
  `udp://@0.0.0.0:6001` as `mpegts`, decode Opus, convert to s16 stereo
  48 kHz with `av.AudioResampler`.
- Output with `PyQt5.QtMultimedia.QAudioOutput` (importable in the venv
  on doggoos; **not checked on the Mac**). `sounddevice` is not installed
  and would be a new dependency.
- Keep a small queue in the client, and never block the output:
  - target about 80 ms queued before playing;
  - when the queue is empty, play silence and wait until the target is
    back;
  - above about 250 ms, drop the oldest audio down to the target.
- What it replaces: `audio_listener` and its ffplay handling
  (`client.py:944-1123`), the mute restart (`set_audio_muted`,
  `client.py:919-941`: mute becomes a volume change), and the
  silencedetect parsing (measure the level of what is actually played
  instead).
- Expected to fix the delay and the crackle. Expected to fix the silence
  as well, because there is no blocking callback left; that part is
  inferred, since the silence's cause is not proven.
- Tests: `test_client_audio.py` pins the ffplay command, the mute restart
  and two live ffplay sections; it needs rewriting. Add one test for the
  queue rules (empty, target, overflow).
- Size: about an afternoon plus a listening test. Needs a client restart.

**Option 2 — ffplay's `-sync ext`.**
- One flag in the ffplay command (`client.py:997`). With an external
  master clock on a realtime input, ffplay adjusts its clock speed
  (0.900–1.010) to hold the audio packet queue between 2 and 10 packets,
  and stretches or shrinks the audio to follow.
- Expected: a small reserve and a bounded delay, around 0.3 s. From
  reading ffplay's source from memory, not tested.
- Risks: the pitch may wobble, since the correction resamples by up to
  10%. It may not stop the permanent silence, because the callback still
  blocks when the queue is empty.
- Size: about 15 minutes plus listening. Needs a client restart.

**How to judge either one.** Run a session of at least 30 minutes with
sound playing on the host and check §4.6: delay stays under about 0.3 s,
the Mac output level never sits at -120 dB while the host log shows
sound, and no crackle by ear.

### 4.6 How to measure (no code needed)

- **Delay.** The host and the client both run `silencedetect` with the
  same settings (-60 dB, 10 s). The host logs `Audio: … silent for 10s+`
  and `sound on … again` at capture time, with milliseconds. The client
  logs `Audio: stream silent for 10s+` and `sound again` when ffplay's
  filter processes the frame, which is after ffplay's packet queue and a
  few tenths of a second before the speaker. Client time minus host time
  for the same event is the delay. The client log has 1 s resolution;
  adding milliseconds to its format would make this ±50 ms.
- **What really reaches the headphones.** `coreaudiod` writes an output
  level line every 10 s. On the Mac:
  `/usr/bin/log show --last 30m --style compact --predicate 'process ==
  "coreaudiod"' | grep RTAID | grep -- '-Output'`. Each line ends in
  `rms:[…], peaks:[…]` in dB; -120 is digital silence. This is an
  undocumented log line; it followed mute, unmute and every stream
  silence in this session.
- **Is the player starved or backed up.** `sample <ffplay pid> 2 10` on
  the Mac. It suspends ffplay for milliseconds and can click.
- **Host sample rate.** Read `wivrn.sink.monitor` with `parec` for a few
  minutes and fit bytes against `time.monotonic()`.

## 5. Findings

Numbered so they can be picked ("fix 1, 3 and 5"). Finding 0 is §4.

### Must fix

**0. Audio output crackles, then goes silent, and the delay is unbounded**
(`client.py:944-1123`). See §4.

**1. ⌘⇧M mutes the stream instead of reaching the host**
(`client.py:3003`)
- **What this is:** the macOS menu item "Mute Host Audio" has the
  shortcut `Ctrl+Shift+M`, which Qt maps to ⌘⇧M.
- **Problem:** the client sends ⌘ to the host as Ctrl
  (`client.py:2491-2495`), so ⌘⇧M meant for a host app (Discord's mute
  toggle, for example) mutes the stream on the Mac and never reaches the
  host. It stays muted until toggled back.
- **Evidence (verified):** `Audio muted` at Nov 10 21:24:14, no `Audio
  unmuted` until Danny did it on Nov 13 00:14:54; ffplay ran with
  `-volume 0` for 50.7 h. The Nov 3 run reports `muted 33h54m` of 34h15m.
  That the key press caused it is **inferred**; a menu click leaves the
  same log line.
- **Fix:** delete the `setShortcut` line. The menu item stays.
- **If we skip it:** sound is lost again at the next press, with one INFO
  line as the only trace.

**2. The Mac runs old client code** — see §3.
- **Fix:** push `ba710e8`, `git pull --rebase` on the Mac, restart the
  client.
- **If we skip it:** fixes keep landing on one machine only.

**3. A stopped screen share stays black until the host restarts**
(`portal_capture.py:197`, `host.py:3118`)
- **What this is:** the host negotiates one portal ScreenCast session and
  keeps it for its lifetime.
- **Problem:** `ensure()` returns at once while `self.session` is set. If
  the session ends (someone clicks "stop sharing", KWin or the portal
  restarts), the feeder dies, the stream retires after 5 restarts
  (`host.py:1298-1303`), and every later start reuses the dead session.
  **Read from code, not reproduced**: reproducing it would have broken
  the live session.
- **Fix:** when a video stream retires in portal mode, call
  `portal._reset_session()` so the next start negotiates again with the
  saved restore token. About 3 lines and one test.
- **If we skip it:** a black screen that needs SSH to the host.

### Should fix

**4. Nothing restarts the host if it crashes** (`autostart_host.py`,
`run.sh`)
- **Problem:** the host starts from the launcher or a login autostart
  entry. The running host is a child of `start.py`. After a crash (a Qt
  abort happened on Oct 19) it stays down.
- **Fix:** a systemd user service with `Restart=on-failure`.
- **If we skip it:** one crash locks the Mac out until someone is at the
  box.

**5. One status line fills the host logs** (`host.py:3701`)
- **Problem:** `[MONITOR] CPU …` is written every 6 s at INFO: 160,425 of
  about 162,000 lines in `host-launch.log`. `host.log` (4 × 2 MB) covers
  about 5 days because of it. `host-launch.log` has no dates and no size
  limit (11.6 MB in 12 days).
- **Fix:** `logging.debug` for that line; add the date to the launch log
  format.
- **If we skip it:** evidence rotates away or cannot be dated.

**6. Client silence times are wrong after a host audio restart**
(`client.py:881-886`)
- **Problem:** the length comes from stream timestamps, which go back to
  0 when the host restarts its encoder. One run reported `silent 246%`; a
  2m01s silence was logged as 4h59m.
- **Fix:** use the wall clock (`now - st["silent_since"]`) and delete the
  regex. Moot if option 1 replaces this code.

### Nice to have

**7. The 5-second "no data" timeout does not exist** (`client.py:994`,
`client.py:2950`)
- **Problem:** both URLs carry `rw_timeout=5000000`, and comments say a
  dead stream then fails and restarts. ffmpeg's UDP input ignores the
  option. **Verified** on ffmpeg n9.0.2: with `rw_timeout=1000000` no
  exit in 6 s; with `timeout=1000000` it exits with an I/O error.
- **Fix:** delete the option and both comments. Do not switch video to
  `timeout=`: video is quiet on a still screen and would reconnect in a
  loop.

**8. The stream restart counter never resets** (`host.py:1280-1305`)
- **Problem:** `failures` is kept for the life of the thread, so 5
  failures spread over a week count as "5 times in a row" and the wait
  grows to 16 s.
- **Fix:** set `failures = 0` when the encoder ran for more than 60 s.

### Checked and found fine

- Host audio: source following (`_audio_watch`) moved the capture at
  every default-sink change in the logs, and the record stream followed
  `wivrn.sink` when WiVRn re-created it.
- `seqrelay.py`: ordering rules read correctly; no finding.
- Handshake, control and upload: token checks with `hmac.compare_digest`,
  bounded reads, PIN lockout per address, upload names and sizes checked.
  All listeners bind every interface and the session token travels in
  clear UDP, which is acceptable only because the client connects over
  Tailscale.
- Mac memory and sockets: ffplay's footprint was flat at 14 MB; `Recv-Q`
  0 in every sample; the system-wide `373478 dropped due to full socket
  buffers` did not move during the checks and is not attributed to a
  socket.
